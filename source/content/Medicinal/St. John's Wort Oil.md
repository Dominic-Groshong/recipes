> [!info] Dosage
> Apply to the affected area of skin 2-3 times a day
### Warning
>[!warning] Caution
> - St. John's wort has many serious interactions with medications. Before taking research any possible interactions with your medications.
> - Theoretically can cause photosensitivity of the skin for some people when applied externally or consumed internally. 
> - Possibly can cause emotional reactions from use, such as anger.
> - Low dosage over a long period of time is recommend to notice side effects. Ex. 5 drops of tincture per day, for 3 months before increasing dosage.



Quantity: 1 ½ cups

## Ingredients 
- [ ] 2 cups - fresh st. john's wort flowers and buds
- [ ] 1 ½ cups - extra virgin olive oil or other oil of your choice (more as needed)
---
## Directions
Loosely pack the flowers and buds into a 16 oz jar, filling it about ¼ inch from the top. Use a utensil to push the plant matter down and allow air bubbles to come up.

Cut squares of cheesecloth to fit over the top of the jar and secure the cloth by screwing the rim of the canning lid onto the jar. This opening will allow the moisture in the plant matter to evaporate.

For the solar-infusion method, the sun needs to be shining. Place your jar in paper bag and set it out in the warm sun each day for a few hours at a time, until the oil becomes saturated with the reddish/burgundy color. This process can take 4-6 weeks.

If solar-infusion is not possible, use a hot-water-bath method. Place the jar in a slow cooker filled with water. The water should reach the level of the oil in the jar. Do not allow the cheesecloth on the top of the jar to touch the water or it could draw more moisture into the oil. 

Put the slow cooker on the lowest setting, and test the temperature of the water periodically to make sure it does not exceed 120 degrees F. Slow cookers with a "warm" setting is ideal. You would likely need to turn the slow cooker on and off to maintain the ideal temperature. 

Let the jar sit in the water of the slow cooker for 48 hours, or until the oil is a deep red. Once finished, strain the plant material using a cheesecloth and put the oil in a clean 16 oz jar. After a day, the sediment from the oil will settle. Decant the oil, and discard the sediment. This extra step makes the oil last longer. Bottle and keep in a cool, dark place for up to 1 year. Storage in the fridge will make it last longer.
